**Paper.** *A ConvNet for the 2020s* — [CVPR 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.03545)

## Why I read it

After Swin Transformer, I wanted to know whether the field's shift toward attention-based backbones for vision was actually load-bearing, or whether a well-modernized convolutional network could close the gap. This paper is exactly that test, run rigorously.

## What the paper claims

Starting from a standard ResNet, the authors incrementally apply a series of design changes borrowed from Transformer-based architectures — larger kernel sizes, fewer activation and normalization layers, depthwise convolutions, an inverted bottleneck, and training-recipe modernization — tracking accuracy at each step. The resulting "ConvNeXt," built entirely from standard convolutions with no self-attention, matches or exceeds Swin Transformer's accuracy and scaling behavior across classification, detection, and segmentation benchmarks.

## What convinced me

The step-by-step ablation, crediting each individual change with its own accuracy delta, is the strongest part of the paper: it turns "is attention necessary" from a vague architectural debate into a decomposed, checkable accounting of exactly which changes closed most of the gap (training recipe and macro design choices, it turns out, do a lot of the work; the self-attention mechanism specifically does not appear to be the decisive ingredient).

## What it leaves open

The paper's ablations are all on the same natural-image benchmarks as Swin; whether the specific gains — larger kernels, depthwise convolutions — translate the same way to medical images with different scale and texture statistics isn't tested here. It also doesn't settle whether Swin's advantages on tasks *outside* these benchmarks (long-range dependency modeling, for instance) might still favor attention in some settings.

## What I take from it

This paper is a useful corrective to backbone-choice papers that frame "we switched from CNN to Transformer" as inherently meaningful. Given how much of ConvNeXt's gain traces to training-recipe and macro-design changes rather than attention itself, when I see a medical-imaging paper attribute an accuracy improvement to "using a Transformer backbone," I now ask whether the comparison controlled for training recipe — a fair architecture comparison needs to hold training details fixed, and this paper is direct evidence of how much those details alone can move the needle.
